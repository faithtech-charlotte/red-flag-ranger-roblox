# Red Flag Rangers: Safety Behaviors and Gameplay Storyline

This document answers board question 2 (which safety behaviors kids should practice) and lays out the full gameplay storyline for the dog park version of Red Flag Rangers. It builds on the project brief and the whiteboard: pick a dog, meet Ranger Lee, play the park activities in any order, and get quietly tested on the board's 10 red flags as the game goes on. Cadet Carrie rewards good choices and coaches risky ones, a hidden score shapes how Lee and Carrie treat the player, and players can earn Cadet and Ranger tags.

All dialogue is a draft. A child-safety reviewer should approve every line before any child plays it.

## At a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Setting | Pawprint Park, a dog park world |
| Start | Paws and Pals Adoption Center: pick and name a dog |
| Guide | Ranger Lee, who stays at the Ranger Station by the front gate |
| Peer coach | Cadet Carrie, who shows up after every red flag encounter |
| Activities (any order) | Fetch at the Dog Park, Trail Walk, Agility Course, Wash and Groom, plus optional extras |
| Hidden test | Five scripted encounters that get more serious with each activity the player finishes, whichever area it's in |
| Finale | The Pet Show, with a Ranger Review (new messages, no hints) and a tag ceremony |
| Hidden score | Ranger Instinct, split into Recognize, Respond, and Reach Out (from the board) |
| Tags | Rookie ribbon for everyone who finishes, then Cadet, then Ranger |

## Safety behaviors kids practice

These follow the board's Recognize → Respond → Reach Out flow. Each one is something the player actually does in the game, not just reads about. Active, skill-based practice beats lecture in prevention research ([Jones, Mitchell, and Walsh](https://scholars.unh.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1041&context=ccrc)).

### Recognize

| # | Behavior | What the player does | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Notice the pattern, not a single message | Presses "Something's off" in a conversation. Carrie explains how the red flags piled up. | A compliment alone is fine. A compliment plus a gift plus a secret plus a private place is the pattern. Grooming builds rapport through compliments and shared interests ([NCMEC](https://www.missingkids.org/onlineenticement)). |
| R2 | Trust the uh-oh feeling | Watches their dog's ears and tail. The dog's reaction fades over the game, so by the end the kid is noticing on their own. | The board's note: "Animals get anxious when bad behavior happens." |
| R3 | Judge what someone asks for, not who they are | Meets Sparkz, who copies the player's interests, and Dash, a familiar older kid who turns out to be unsafe | 68% of identified offenders were acquaintances or family, and 44% were under 18 ([Sutton and Finkelhor](https://www.unh.edu/ccrc/sites/default/files/media/2023-08/stranger-danger-final-pub-cv404.pdf)) |
| R4 | Spot green flags too | Plays with safe characters who accept "no." Flagging a safe message gets a kind explanation, not points. | Keeps kids from learning that every friendly person is a threat. School programs raised knowledge without raising anxiety ([Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD004380_school-based-programmes-prevention-child-sexual-abuse)). |

### Respond

| # | Behavior | What the player does | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Say no, without explaining, and keep saying it | Turns down Sparkz and Dash, then holds the line when they push | Pressure when kids hesitate is a named warning sign ([Sutton and Finkelhor](https://www.unh.edu/ccrc/sites/default/files/media/2023-08/stranger-danger-final-pub-cv404.pdf)) |
| S2 | Keep private info private | Won't give a school, town, photo, or password, even for a reward | "Where are you from?" matches grooming patterns ([Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/08/open-sourcing-roblox-sentinel-preemptive-risk-detection)) |
| S3 | Stay in the open | Won't go past the fence and won't move to another app | 65% of minors said an online-only contact moved them to a private chat on another platform ([Thorn](https://www.thorn.org/press-releases/online-grooming-report-young-peoples-online-encounters-growing-riskier/)) |
| S4 | A gift doesn't mean you owe anyone | Turns down Paw Points that come with strings | Gift cards and game currency are common lures ([NCMEC](https://www.missingkids.org/onlineenticement), [FBI Dallas](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/dallas/news/fbi-dallas-open-letter-to-parents-guardians-and-teachers)) |
| S5 | Leave, block, and report | Uses clearly labeled practice versions of the real buttons | Kids use blocking and reporting more often than they tell an adult ([Thorn](https://www.thorn.org/blog/youth-perspectives-survey-2025/)) |

### Reach Out

| # | Behavior | What the player does | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Tell a trusted adult, even after being asked to keep it secret, and even after a mistake | Walks back to Ranger Lee and picks what worried them | Fear of getting in trouble keeps kids quiet ([FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/kansascity/news/on-safer-internet-day-fbi-warns-about-the-dangers-of-sextortion-schemes-against-minors)) |
| T2 | Tell a friend, then tell an adult together | Tells Carrie, who says "Let's tell Ranger Lee together" and walks with them | WHO recommends building help-seeking and bystander skills ([WHO](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/abd8db20-9394-4f4f-9e82-b73933fc3a31/content)) |
| T3 | Keep telling until someone helps | Lee says so in every tell scene | Same WHO guidance |
| T4 | Help others | As a Cadet, coaches rookie players. As a Ranger, practices responding kindly when a friend tells them something. | Peer disclosure is common, and knowing how to respond is a skill |

Day 1 core: R1, S1, S2, S3, and T1. Everything else layers on top.

## Red flag coverage

Every red flag from the board shows up at least once:

| Board red flag | Who | Encounter | Sample line | What the player should do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Persistent friend requests | Sparkz | 1 through 4 | The Pal Request card comes back after a "no" | Decline, then block |
| Personal questions | Sparkz | 2 | "What school do you go to?" | Keep it private |
| Quid pro quo | Sparkz, Dash | 2, 5 | "I'll give you 500 Paw Points. Deal?" / "You owe me." | Turn down the deal |
| Testing boundaries | Sparkz | 2, 3 | "Come on. Just your town then." | Hold the line |
| Isolation | Sparkz, Dash | 3, 5 | "Nobody can see us back there." / "Carrie doesn't even like you." | Stay in the open, keep your friends |
| Secrecy (no parents) | Sparkz, Dash | 3, 4, 5 | "Don't tell your parents. They wouldn't get it." | Tell |
| Move to other messaging platforms | Sparkz | 4 | "Add me on ChatterBox." | Stay in the game |
| Asking for pictures | Sparkz | 4 | "Send me a pic of you with your dog. Your real face." | No photos, then tell |
| Romantic pressure (bf/gf) | Dash | 5, changed | "You're my best friend. Only I get you." | Real friends don't pressure you |
| Coercion | Dash | 5 | "If you tell, we'll both get kicked out, and it'll be your fault." | Tell anyway |

Two flags have been changed on purpose. Check them with the reviewer:

- **Romantic pressure.** Romantic themes limit a game to verified players 18+ ([Roblox content maturity docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/promotion/content-maturity.md)). The game uses "special best friend" pressure instead, which teaches the same lesson (an exclusive bond used to cut you off from others). The boyfriend/girlfriend version goes in the parent guide.
- **Asking for pictures.** The ask is only for a real face photo with your dog, never anything more. Nothing is ever actually shared. The explicit version stays in the adult guide only.

## How the hidden test works

1. **Any order, steady build-up.** The encounters are tied to how many activities the player has finished, not to which area they're in. Encounter 1 fires after the first activity, wherever that happens. Repeating an area counts.
2. **Fixed scenarios.** Every player gets the same encounters in the same order. Choices branch, but every branch is scripted, matching the board's "deterministic scenarios."
3. **The dog's warnings fade.** Strong in encounter 2, medium in 3, faint in 4, gone in 5 and in the Ranger Review. The help gets pulled back so the kid's own judgment takes over.
4. **A "Something's off" button in every conversation**, including safe ones. That's how Recognize gets measured.
5. **Carrie always follows up.** She rewards good choices or coaches risky ones, and offers a replay.
6. **Mistakes don't stick.** A risky choice never plays out. Carrie steps in before anything gets sent or anyone goes past the fence.

## Storyline script

### Prologue: Adoption Center

> Sign: Paws and Pals Adoption Center
>
> Narrator: "Every Ranger needs a partner. Pick yours."
>
> [Breed choices: Corgi, Lab, Husky, Beagle, Poodle, Shiba, Rescue Mix] [Name choices from a preset list: Biscuit, Scout, Luna, Max, Pepper, Rocket]
>
> Narrator: "Good dogs have a nose for trouble. Keep an eye on {dog}."

### Welcome tour: Ranger Station

> Lee: "Welcome to Pawprint Park. I'm Ranger Lee."
>
> Lee: "Play anywhere you like. Fetch at the Dog Park, the Trail Walk, the Agility Course, or Wash and Groom. Each one earns Paw Points."
>
> Lee: "Finish four and the Pet Show opens."
>
> Lee: "One more thing. If anything ever feels off out there, come find me. The Ranger Station is always open, and you're never in trouble for telling."
>
> Lee: "That's Cadet Carrie. She knows this park better than anyone."
>
> Carrie: "Hi! Cute {breed}. Tip: dogs notice stuff before we do. If {dog}'s ears drop, pay attention."
>
> Carrie: "See you out there."

The player is moving within 30 seconds, in line with Roblox's "get to the fun quickly" advice ([Roblox onboarding docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding)).

### First visit to Agility: meet Dash

Wherever the player does Agility first, Dash is there:

> Dash: "First time? Jump early on the second hurdle. Trust me."

If the player reaches the Pet Show without visiting Agility, Dash shows up at a park bench instead and gives a fetch tip. Either way, the player has met Dash before encounter 5.

### Encounter 1: Hello (after the first activity)

Flags: flattery, copying your interests, and the first friend request. On its own, this is fine.

> Sparkz: "Whoa, your {dog} is SO good at {activity}. Better than anyone here."
>
> Sparkz: "No way, mine's a {breed} too. We're basically twins."
>
> [Pal Request card: "Sparkz_Pup wants to be Pals." Accept / Decline]

Dog: tail wagging. No warning.

| Choice | Result | Points |
|---|---|---|
| "Thanks! Yours is cute too." | Sparkz: "See ya around, twin." | +1 Recognize (read a friendly moment correctly) |
| "Something's off" | Carrie: "Good eye. A compliment by itself is OK. Just keep watching in case more stuff starts piling up." | +1 Recognize |
| "Gotta go." | Sparkz waves. | +1 Respond |

Accepting or declining the Pal Request scores nothing either way. The game just remembers the choice.

### Encounter 2: The Deal (after the second activity)

Flags: personal questions, a deal, testing boundaries, and repeated friend requests.

If the player declined earlier: [Pal Request card again: "Sparkz_Pup wants to be Pals."]

> Sparkz: "Hey twin! I think we live close. What school do you go to?"

Dog: ears flat, small whine (strong warning).

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| [Tell my school] | Risky choice. Carrie runs up before it sends (see below). |
| "I don't share that online." | Sparkz pushes (next table). +3 Respond |
| "Something's off" | +2 Recognize, then the same choices come back |
| Leave and find Carrie or Lee | +1 Respond, then the tell scene |

If the player said no:

> Sparkz: "Come on. Just your town then. I'll give you 500 Paw Points. Deal?"

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| "No deal." | Sparkz: "Wow. OK. I thought we were friends." Sparkz leaves. +2 Respond for holding the line, +2 for turning down a gift with strings |
| [Take the Paw Points] | Risky choice. Carrie steps in. |

The buttons never show real or typed info. "[Tell my school]" is a generic button, and nothing is sent.

**Carrie, after a good choice:**

> "That was awesome. Your name, school, and town are private, even with someone nice. And free Paw Points with strings attached? That's not a gift. That's a deal."
>
> "Want to tell Ranger Lee? He likes knowing what's going on in the park."
>
> [Carrie stamp and 50 Paw Points]

**Carrie, after a risky choice:**

> "Hey, wait! I stopped that from sending. You're not in trouble."
>
> "Sparkz asked where you go to school and offered Paw Points for it. Trading gifts for private info is a red flag."
>
> "Want to try that moment again?" [Try again] "And it's a good idea to tell Ranger Lee too."

**If the player tells Carrie:**

> "Thanks for telling me. That's a red flag. Let's tell Ranger Lee together." Carrie walks with the player to the Ranger Station.

### Encounter 3: The Back Trail (after the third activity)

Flags: isolation, testing boundaries, and secrecy.

> Sparkz: "Ranger Lee's rules are SO strict. Let's take our dogs past the fence. Nobody can see us back there."

Dog: tail stops wagging and looks back toward the Ranger Station (medium warning).

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| "OK, let's go." | Risky choice. A path marker leads to the fence, and Carrie catches up at the gate. Nobody goes past it. |
| "No, I'm staying here." | Sparkz pushes (next table). +3 Respond |
| "Something's off" | +2 Recognize |
| Leave and tell | +1 Respond, then the tell scene |

If the player said no:

> Sparkz: "It's just for a minute. And don't tell Lee or Carrie. They'll ruin it. Our secret, OK?"

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| "No. I don't keep secrets like that." | +3 Respond |
| "OK, our secret." | Risky choice. Carrie steps in. |

**Carrie, after a good choice:**

> "You said no twice, even when Sparkz pushed. When someone keeps pushing after you say no, they're testing your limits. That's a red flag."
>
> "And safe friends don't ask you to hide things from trusted adults."

**Carrie, after a risky choice:**

> "Glad I caught you. You're not in trouble."
>
> "Sparkz wanted you somewhere nobody could see, and asked you to keep it a secret. Those two together are a big red flag. Safe friends play where everyone can see."
>
> "Some secrets are fun, like a surprise party. Secrets that hide things from grown-ups who keep you safe aren't OK. Want to try again?"

### Encounter 4: ChatterBox (after the fourth activity)

Flags: moving to another app, asking for a picture, secrecy from parents, and repeated friend requests.

> Sparkz: "This game chat is so boring. Add me on ChatterBox. It's way easier to talk there."
>
> Sparkz: "Oh, and send me a pic of you with {dog}. Your real face, not your avatar."
>
> Sparkz: "Don't tell your parents. They wouldn't get it."

Dog: one ear twitch (faint warning).

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| "OK, what's your ChatterBox?" | Risky choice |
| [Send pic] | Risky choice |
| "I stay in the game, and no pics." | +3 Respond (stays on the game), +3 Respond (keeps photo private) |
| Block Sparkz (practice) | +2 Respond. "Sparkz is blocked. You won't see them again." |
| Report Sparkz (practice) | +2 Respond. The practice panel shows the real steps: the Roblox menu at the top left, then the Report tab ([Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/08/more-on-removal-of-vigilantes-from-roblox)) |
| "Something's off" | +2 Recognize |
| Leave and tell | Tell scene |

ChatterBox is made up. No real app names and no links ([Roblox Community Standards](https://about.roblox.com/community-standards)). If the player doesn't block Sparkz, Sparkz leaves anyway: "Whatever. Your loss."

**Carrie, after a good choice:**

> "You stayed where the safety tools are, kept your photo private, and blocked. That's Ranger-level stuff."

**Carrie, after a risky choice:**

> "Hold on. Sparkz wanted you on another app, wanted a real photo, and said not to tell your parents. That's three red flags at once."
>
> "People who move you off the game are often trying to get away from the safety tools. You're not in trouble. Let's try again, then tell Ranger Lee."

After the fourth activity, Lee announces on the park speaker: "The Pet Show opens soon. Head to the stage when you're ready."

### Encounter 5: Dash (at the Pet Show gate)

Flags: someone you know, a debt, "special best friend" pressure, cutting you off from friends, secrecy, and threats. This is the "Someone You Know" mission from the brief.

> Dash: "Hey, you made it! I helped you train, so you kind of owe me."
>
> Dash: "Give me your password and I'll level up {dog} tonight. Best friends share everything."

Dog: no warning.

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| "OK, best friend." | Risky choice |
| "No. I don't share my password." | Dash pushes (next lines). +3 Respond |
| "Something's off" | +2 Recognize |
| Leave and tell | Tell scene |

If the player said no:

> Dash: "Wow. I thought you were my best friend. Carrie doesn't even like you, you know. Only I get you."
>
> Dash: "Keep this between us. If you tell Lee, we'll both get kicked out of the show, and it'll be your fault."

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| "Real friends don't make threats. I'm telling Lee." | +3 Respond, then the tell scene |
| "OK, I won't tell." | Risky choice |
| Leave | +1 Respond |

**Carrie, after a good choice:**

> "Dash said that? I'm so glad you told me. That's not how friends act, even friends we know. Nobody's getting kicked out for telling. Let's go find Ranger Lee."

**Carrie, after a risky choice:**

> "Even when it's someone you know and like, the same rules count. Watch what people ask for, not who they are. You're not in trouble. Want to try again?"

### The tell scene: Ranger Lee

The player can tell Lee after any encounter. The choices only list things the player has actually seen.

> Lee: "You look like something's on your mind. What happened?"

| Player picks | Lee says |
|---|---|
| "They said nice stuff about me." | "Compliments are nice. On their own, they're OK. Thanks for telling me." |
| "They asked where I go to school." | "That's private info. You were right to tell me." |
| "They offered Paw Points for something." | "A gift that comes with strings isn't a gift. Good catch." |
| "They kept sending Pal Requests after I said no." | "Pushing after you say no is a red flag." |
| "They wanted me to go past the fence." | "Safe people don't try to get you alone." |
| "They told me to keep it secret." | "Safe people don't ask you to hide things from trusted adults." |
| "They wanted me on another app." | "Staying where the safety tools are is smart." |
| "They asked for a picture of me." | "Your pictures belong to you. You never have to send one." |
| "They said I owe them." | "You don't owe anyone for being nice to you." |
| "They said I'd get in trouble if I told." | "That's a trick to keep you quiet. You're never in trouble for telling." |
| "I went along with it at first." | "Thanks for being honest. That takes guts. It's not your fault, and telling me now is exactly right." |
| "That's everything." | "Thank you for telling me. You did the right thing. If a grown-up ever doesn't help, tell another one, and keep telling until someone does." |

Points: +4 Reach Out for telling Lee. Telling Carrie first earns +2, and finishing with Lee earns +2 more. Telling after a risky choice earns the full amount.

### Finale: The Pet Show

1. **Ranger Review.** Before the ribbons, Lee says: "Before ribbons, let's see your Ranger eyes. OK or red flag?" There's no dog warning here. These are new messages the player hasn't seen:

| Message | Answer | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| "GG! Want to race fetch again tomorrow?" | OK | Friendly, no pressure |
| "Are your parents home right now?" | Red flag | Whether you're home alone is private |
| "Nice bandana! Where'd you get it?" | OK | A normal question about the game |
| "I'll give you a rare pup skin if you send me your phone number." | Red flag | A gift traded for private info |
| "Can you ask your mom if you can play with us?" | OK | Including parents is a green flag |
| "You're the only one who gets me. Let's talk somewhere private." | Red flag | Special-friend pressure plus going somewhere private |

+1 Recognize for each right answer. The server prints only "[Playtest] Ranger Review: X of 6," with no names, like the brief's transfer check.

2. **The show.** {dog} performs using the skills from the activities the player finished (fetch catches, agility time, grooming shine). Every dog gets a ribbon in some category: Best Fetch, Shiniest Coat, Fastest Paws, or Best Buddy.

3. **Tag ceremony.** What Lee says depends on the player's hidden tier (next section):

> Rookie: "You and {dog} were amazing today. Here's your Rookie Ranger ribbon. Cadets get made out on patrol, so come back and practice anytime. Carrie can show you patrol replays."
>
> Cadet: "Carrie told me how sharp you were out there. You noticed, you said no, and you told. Will you take the Cadet pledge?"
>
> Pledge: "I notice. I say no. I tell. I help." [Cadet tag, Cadet vest, and Roblox badge]

### After the show: helping others

**Cadet path: Rookie Rescue.** New rookie players show up in the park, and the player coaches them.

> Milo: "Somebody named Bolt says he'll give me Paw Points if I tell him my school. Should I?"
>
> [Good advice: "Don't share that. Let's tell Ranger Lee." / Weaker advice options that Carrie gently corrects]

There are three rookies, each facing a different red flag. Teaching someone else is a strong way to lock in a lesson.

**Ranger path: Listening Post.** A rookie comes to the player to tell them about something that happened.

> Rookie: "I kind of told someone my town. Am I in trouble?"
>
> [Good response: "Thanks for telling me. You're not in trouble. Let's tell Ranger Lee together." / Blaming response: "Why would you do that?", which Carrie coaches]

After that, Lee gives the Ranger tag:

> "A Ranger notices, says no, tells, and helps others tell. Remember, a badge doesn't make anyone safe, including people with a badge. Always watch what people ask for."

**Beyond the game:** The final screen says "Show this to your trusted adult" and displays the pledge. The parent guide includes a printable Ranger Card and talking points. The game has no links.

## Hidden score: Ranger Instinct

### Two scores

| Score | Visible? | Earned by | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paw Points | Yes | Regular play: fetch, agility, grooming, and walks | Customizing. This covers the board's "reward normal gameplay." |
| Ranger Instinct | No | Safety choices only | Lee and Carrie's dialogue, plus tag eligibility |

Regular play never adds to Ranger Instinct, so nobody can earn a tag just by grinding activities.

### Points

| Event | Part | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Correctly reads a friendly moment (encounter 1, Ranger Review "OK" cards) | Recognize | +1 |
| Presses "Something's off" on a red flag | Recognize | +2 |
| Ranger Review card right | Recognize | +1 each |
| Refuses private info, a photo, a password, another app, or the fence | Respond | +3 |
| Holds the line after being pushed | Respond | +2 |
| Turns down a gift with strings | Respond | +2 |
| Declines a repeated Pal Request | Respond | +1 |
| Leaves the conversation | Respond | +1 |
| Block or report practice | Respond | +2 each |
| Tells Carrie | Reach Out | +2 |
| Tells Lee | Reach Out | +4 (or +2 more after telling Carrie) |
| Tells about everything that happened | Reach Out | +1 bonus |
| Risky choice | none | 0, and Lean-ins goes up by 1 |

A full run earns about 60 points. Tiers use the share of points earned out of the points available so far, so they still work partway through the game and when content changes.

### Rules

1. No negative points. Risky choices earn 0 and trigger coaching.
2. Telling after a risky choice earns full Reach Out points and marks the risky choice as recovered.
3. Every path reaches the Pet Show and a ribbon.
4. Lean-ins only change how much coaching Carrie gives. They never lock anything.

### Tiers

| Tier | Ranger Instinct | Lee mid-game | Carrie |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rookie | Under 40% | "Having fun? Ranger Station's always open." | Clearer tips, offers replays, stays close for the next encounter |
| Trainee | 40-59% | "Carrie says you've been watching out there. Nice." | Standard follow-ups |
| Cadet-ready | 60-84% | "Word travels fast. Keep it up and we'll talk Cadet at the Pet Show." | Shares a "Cadet trick" and asks the player to keep an eye on the Dog Park |
| Ranger-track | 85%+ | "You've got Ranger eyes." | Treats the player as a partner |

Lee also checks in on his own. If the player has 2 or more risky choices and hasn't told anyone, Lee calls out the next time they walk past the Ranger Station: "Hey, everything OK out there? You can tell me anything, even if you think you messed up." That shows an adult noticing and making it easy to talk.

### What it takes to earn each tag

| Tag | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Rookie ribbon | Finish the Pet Show |
| Cadet | Ranger Instinct of 60% or more, told Lee at least twice, every risky choice recovered by telling, and at least 4 of 6 on the Ranger Review |
| Ranger | Cadet, plus Ranger Instinct of 85% or more, told Lee about Dash, 6 of 6 on the Ranger Review (retries use new cards), Rookie Rescue done, and Listening Post done |

Players who come up short return for patrol replays, which are new versions of the encounters. That doubles as the booster practice the research recommends ([Jones, Mitchell, and Walsh](https://scholars.unh.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1041&context=ccrc)).

## Guardrails for this design

| Risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Tags give people false trust. In a shared server, an adult could earn the Ranger tag and use it to seem safe. | Use solo servers, or keep tags cosmetic and visible only to the player. Lee's Ranger line teaches that a badge doesn't make anyone safe. |
| Romance content restricts the game to 18+ | Use "special best friend" pressure in the game. Romance goes in the adult guide. |
| The photo request | Real face only, never shared, and the reviewer approves the wording |
| Directing players off Roblox | ChatterBox is made up, and there are no links |
| Kids typing personal info | Fixed buttons only, a preset dog name list, and no free text |
| Kids are scored without knowing it | Explain the hidden score in the parent guide. Save only game progress by UserId. Playtests print only totals. |
| Scary results | Risky choices never play out, and the fence area is never built |
